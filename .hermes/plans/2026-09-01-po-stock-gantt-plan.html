1Why the current check can't say "40% of the run"

Today's stock check is a snapshot with no clock. It explodes each block's recipe and compares that block's need against the whole on-hand pile, on its own. Two blocks drawing the same sleeve both see the full pile. Two demand weeks see the same number. There is no order of consumption, no depletion hour, and no notion of anything arriving.

4

0

So the request needs two additions rather than a tweak. First, a timeline per component: opening stock, minus each block's draw spread across its run, plus receipts stepping in on their dates. Second, an inbound feed: the PO report. Everything Carsten sees on the board falls out of that one curve.

Day-one effect

Netting alone changes verdicts before any PO data exists. On today's board the second 280591 piece on P22 exhausts film 752706 at 84% of its run, and the long 280324 run on P21 exhausts strawberry puree 750131 at 31%. Both are green today. Expect a few surprises the first morning, and a popover that names the other block drawing the same item.

2What the PO report gives us

The sample joins to the plant's data more cleanly than expected. The columns we use: Order number, Ordered item, Qty ordered at the origin, its unit, Receipt date, Initial Receipt Date, Arrival area, Supplier, and Receipt number (blank means not yet received).

Two joins, one authoritative

  • Item to recipe: the PO item code is the BOM item code. No suffix games, no unit conversion.
  • PO to dock appointment: the Shipping/Receiving sheet stores the last 8 digits of the ERP order number. That pairs a dock time-of-day with the ERP date, but only for packaging carriers, and only 8 to 11 days ahead. The ERP date is authoritative; the appointment confirms and adds a clock time. It never gates a verdict, with one exception: SL3 lines are an offsite warehouse and only count once a transfer appointment exists.

Traps the sample exposed

  • Vintage mismatch. The 8/24 extract against the 9/1 stock export: 43 of 79 lines had already landed and were on hand. Adding PO quantity to on-hand would double count. Rule: a line counts only if its receipt date is on or after the stock export day, and packaging lines are suppressed as "landed" when matching lots with a decoded batch date already sit in stock.
  • Overdue lines. Ten lines were past their date and unreceipted. Three landed late, three had landed early without paperwork, three never appeared. Overdue means unknown, never "arriving today".
  • Untracked consumables. Ink additive, CHEP pallets and stretch film are on every recipe and on the PO, but have no stock rows. They stay out of the verdict unless you decide otherwise.
Ask IT for

A fixed filename. An explicit as-of timestamp in the file. A daily run in the same job as the stock export. A receipt window of at least 5 weeks. Columns for quantity received and quantity remaining, so partial receipts drop out cleanly. Standing blanket POs included (the weekly CAPS orders never appear in the extract). A legend for the arrival areas RB1, RP1, AMB, SL3, RC1. Confirmation of when the ERP backflushes consumption: at declaration or at MO close.

3The risk model

One balance curve per component along the board's hour axis. Opening stock is the toggle-honouring on-hand from the VIF export. Every production block draws its recipe need uniformly across its run, clipped at the export moment for running MOs. Receipts step the curve up on their ready hour. From that curve, three questions are asked for each block.

  • Hard: does on-hand alone last through the run?
  • Reliable: counting only receipts that land at least 4 days before this block starts, does it last?
  • Planned: counting every receipt in the window, does it last?

OK

On-hand covers the run, or every receipt it needs lands 4 or more days before start. The second case shows a grey truck: "backed".

No warning

Delivery-dependent

The run completes only thanks to a receipt landing inside the buffer, or mid-run. This is your shipping-constraint flag.

Orange chip, banner on drop, Reconcile warning

Short

Even with every receipt counted, the line stops at a depletion hour. The chip shows the covered share.

Red chip; blocking in Reconcile on a fresh feed

No data

The shortfall falls beyond the PO window, or the feed is missing or older than a week. Grey question mark.

Never alarms, never comforts

How the 4-day rule reads in the awkward cases

  • Buffer measured to block start, your sentence. The depletion moment is shown too, so Carsten sees both "lands 1.3 days before start" and "runs out Friday 23:20".
  • Partial dependency. The buffer applies only to receipts the curve actually needs. If on-hand covers the whole run, tomorrow's truck is irrelevant. If the dependent share is under 5%, it is information, not a warning.
  • Stacked deliveries. Each needed receipt has its own lead; the tightest one is reported, and "safe from" is that receipt's ready hour plus 4 days.
  • Shared crossings. When a running MO and a planner block both draw the item across the depletion hour, both are flagged. The locked one gets the action "chase PO"; the movable one gets "move to safe from".
  • Warn-only by default. No drop, resize, place or edit is refused. A hard block exists as a config switch and, if ever on, fires only for zero on-hand and zero inbound on a fresh feed.

5Architecture

The rule lives in one pure Python module and one line-for-line TypeScript port, with a parity test between them. The existing flat statuses, the DNS trim and the solver are untouched; the report gains additive keys.

New

  • code/stockcheck/po_import.py: the xlsx reader, best-effort, errors collected.
  • code/stockcheck/timeline.py: gate receipts, build timelines, evaluate a block or the board, earliest clear start, verdict text.
  • helpers/calendar_io.build_stock_payload: the table the Gantt receives.
  • frontend/src/utils/stockRisk.ts: the port.
  • Tests for import, timeline goldens, and Python-to-TypeScript parity.

Changed

  • VIF import and the report cache learn a seventh file, so a new PO drop invalidates the shared report. Three file lists must name it.
  • Report gains inbound, per-SKU unit recipes, and a supply block per schedule row. Memoising unit recipes also cuts the report from about 7 s to 3 s.
  • Reconcile engine, data health, Settings, Stock Check, the calendar page and the Gantt wrapper each grow one hook.
  • Bridge scripts gain glob support until IT commits to a fixed filename.
  • Separate enabler commit: split MO pieces share one block id, and three edit paths in the board state patch every piece at once. Verdicts key on id plus start, and that bug gets fixed first.

7Decisions for you

Each comes with the default the plan assumes. Say "defaults" and slice 0 can start; change any one and the plan absorbs it.

  1. Measure the 4 days to block start, or to the moment stock would run out?Block start, your wording. Both numbers are always shown.
  2. One buffer for everything, or longer for imported fruit?4 days for all to begin. Fruit suppliers slipped 9 to 37 days in the sample, so revisit after a month of feeds.
  3. Apply the rule to packaging and raw materials alike?Yes. Per-area readiness handles the difference.
  4. Overdue open lines: never counted, or treated as arriving today?Never counted, always listed.
  5. When is a receipt usable? And do SL3 lines wait for a transfer appointment?Packaging 16:00 on the date, raw plus 72 hours for QC, SL3 only from a transfer appointment. Confirm QC release times with the plant.
  6. Which stock statuses count as on hand?Available only, as today. QC, quarantine and trailer tiers stay opt-in toggles.
  7. Ever refuse a placement?No. Warn only. The switch exists for later.
  8. Reconcile severity?Dependent warns, Short blocks on a fresh feed, No-data never.
  9. Show grey "backed" trucks on blocks that are fine thanks to a delivery?Yes, informational. Revisit after a week.
  10. Let receipts lift the solver's demand trim and add an earliest-start constraint?After slices 0 to 2 have run a week. Separate sign-off.
  11. Ink, CHEP pallets, stretch film: keep them out of the verdict, or ask IT to export their stock?Keep them never-gating for now.
  12. Commit a trimmed real PO sample as a test fixture, or synthetic only?Synthetic only. Supplier names stay out of git.
  13. Deliver the file through the bridge under a fixed name, or add an upload box?Bridge, fixed name. Upload box only if IT emails the file instead.

8What the flag won't know, and how it says so

  • The dangerous direction is false comfort. A future-dated line that already landed counts twice. The landed rule catches packaging lots by batch date; aseptic purees and IQF fruit are only marked unverifiable. IT's as-of stamp and quantity-remaining column are the real fix.
  • Consumption within a run is assumed uniform. A block that only needs an item late in the run cannot be expressed yet.
  • A truck on the yard that isn't receipted reads as not arrived. Conservative, and captioned.
  • Partial and split receipts: received lines drop out entirely until IT adds quantity remaining.
  • Holding cards are judged alone. Five cards of one SKU each read "safe from Wednesday" although together they exhaust the item. The card says so.
  • The stock export moment is the laptop pull time, up to 35 minutes late. Running-MO clipping is off by that much times the rate. Small, and shown in the caption.
  • Until slice 3, two kilogram conventions coexist: the flat status uses rate times hours, the supply verdict uses the block's own kilograms. The popover says which.
  • The sample's window ends 8/29, so on today's data most shortfalls read No data, not Short. That is correct behaviour, and the reason slice 0 comes first.
